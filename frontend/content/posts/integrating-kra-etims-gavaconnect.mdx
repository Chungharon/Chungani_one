If you integrated KRA eTIMS a year ago, you called the sandbox directly with your
device credentials and that was it. As of 2026 that path is gone: KRA now fronts
the eTIMS sandbox with the **GavaConnect** enterprise gateway (`developer.go.ke`),
and most integration guides online haven't caught up.

> **TL;DR** — There are now **two** independent auth layers, and they are *not*
> interchangeable. A GavaConnect **bearer token** authorises your *app* to reach
> the API. Your eTIMS **`cmcKey`** authorises your *device*. **Every call carries
> both.** If your old code sent only the device credentials, that's why it's
> failing now.

## The two layers

```
Your app ── consumer key/secret ──▶ GavaConnect ── Bearer token ──▶ eTIMS API
Your device ── TIN + bhfId + serial ──▶ device init ── cmcKey ──▶ signed onto every call
```

| Layer | Authorises | Credential | Sent as |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gateway** (GavaConnect) | Your *app* | consumer key + secret → bearer token | `Authorization: Bearer <token>` |
| **Device** (eTIMS OSCU) | Your *device* | TIN + bhfId + serial → `cmcKey` | in the eTIMS headers/body |

The bearer token is **not** the `cmcKey`. Keep your existing endpoint *paths* and
device logic — just route through the GavaConnect host and add the token.

## Getting set up (one-time)

1. Sign up at **`developer.go.ke`** and **create an app**.
2. **Subscribe** that app to the **KRA eTIMS Sandbox API** ("KRA-ETIMS-SBX").
3. Copy the app's **consumer key** and **consumer secret**.
4. Note the **token URL** and **invoke base URL** shown on the API page — the
   invoke host may differ from the old `etims-api-sbx.kra.go.ke`.

If you've used Safaricom Daraja, this is the same `client_credentials` flow.

## The token, in practice

Fetch a token with your key/secret as HTTP Basic auth, then attach it to every
eTIMS call:

```
POST <token-url>          # client_credentials grant, key:secret as Basic auth
→ { "access_token": "…", "expires_in": 3600 }

POST <gateway>/<etims-path>
Authorization: Bearer <access_token>      ← gateway layer
{ tin, bhfId, cmcKey, …payload }          ← device layer
```

Tokens are short-lived (commonly ~1 hour), so **cache and refresh** them. On a
gateway `401`, refresh once and retry.

## Things that will bite you

- **Two error channels.** A non-2xx **HTTP** status is usually a gateway/transport
  problem (bad token, app not subscribed). But a **`200 OK` can still be a failure**
  — check `resultCd` in the body (`"000"` = success; anything else, read `resultMsg`).
- **`403` on an eTIMS call** = your app isn't subscribed/approved for the sandbox API.
- **`resultCd 901` ("not a valid device")** = wrong or unregistered device serial.
  Use the exact KRA-approved `dvcSrlNo` — dynamically generated serials are rejected.
- **`invcNo` must be sequential, unique per branch, and never reused** — not even
  after a cancellation. Keep one authoritative counter per branch.
- **Never log the bearer token, consumer secret, or `cmcKey`.** Redact them before
  persisting any request body.

## The payoff

Once both layers line up, a sandbox sale comes back signed — a real invoice
number, signature, internal data, and a verifiable QR string. KRA's sandbox also
exposes an **"Integrator Automated Testing"** tool that feeds the certification
process, so it's worth running your flows there during development, not just at
the end.

The mental model that unlocks all of it: **the gateway authorises your app, the
device key authorises your hardware, and eTIMS wants to see both on every call.**
